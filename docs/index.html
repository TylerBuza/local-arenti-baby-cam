LESS EXPOSURE. MORE CONTROL.

A private room deserves
a smaller attack surface.

Cloud cameras add account logins, remote services and provider-side access to an already sensitive device. Local viewing removes those dependencies from the everyday video path.

01

Fewer accounts in the loop

The viewer uses local camera credentials. No vendor account, cloud signaling or automatic vendor-relay fallback during normal viewing.

02

Your recordings, your choice

Windows saves a screenshot or video only when you ask. Android and the connector stream in memory. No app analytics or advertising.

03

A boundary you enforce

Local-first software doesn’t stop camera firmware from phoning home. Router-level isolation is the second half of the project.

DISCOVER → PAIR → RECONNECT

Local first, from the first run.

  1. Enable the camera’s local interface

    Complete the manufacturer’s initial Wi-Fi setup if needed. Enable password-protected ONVIF/RTSP where your firmware supports it. The local password may differ from your cloud login.

  2. Find it from Windows

    Choose “Find cameras on this network,” select the service URL, enter local credentials and test. Manual URL entry works when discovery is blocked. Credentials are encrypted for your Windows user.

  3. Pair your phone

    On the same LAN, scan the short-lived QR in Windows → Phone access. Your phone gets its own certificate. After pairing, it reconnects automatically. Reserve the camera’s LAN IP for reliable reconnects.

  4. Test, then isolate

    Check local playback and recovery before blocking camera internet access. Keep required LAN access, and verify both IPv4 and IPv6 restrictions.

THE RECOMMENDED NEXT STEP

Let the camera see the room.
Not the internet.

Once setup works, deny the camera’s WAN access while preserving the local connections you actually need.

Block the device, not just a domain

Use camera-specific rules or an isolated VLAN. Cover outbound traffic on all protocols, IPv4 and IPv6. Remove camera port forwards and prevent UPnP mappings. Consider existing sessions, DNS proxying and alternate routes.

Verify the result

Use firewall counters, logs and suitable captures. Check that new external sessions fail while local viewing survives. Retest after reconnects and safe restarts. A saved rule or an “offline” badge is not proof.

Cloud alerts, vendor remote viewing, updates and some clock/sensor features may stop working. Plan maintenance deliberately. The project does not modify your router automatically.

HONEST LIMITS

Local-first isn’t magic.

Trust your LAN

Phone-to-PC uses authenticated TLS. Camera-side RTSP/ONVIF is not encrypted by that TLS connection. Segment and protect your home network.

Remote is optional

LAN-only is the default. Optional VPN/Funnel configuration adds external services and another access boundary. Never expose the camera’s own ports.

This is an early beta

The core viewer has run on one D3. Generic setup is tested with synthetic fixtures; independent users’ hardware remains to be validated. No guaranteed monitoring uptime.
